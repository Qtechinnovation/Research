Home Front Analysis

By Quincy Amberson

In Kristin Hannah's Home Front, Jolene returns from battle scarred, but apparently not scarred enough to get help from the VA. When Jolene calls the VA and asks about going to see a therapist, the response she recieves is "Are you thinking of hurting yourself or others?" (Hannah 313). That is not a responsible or reasonable standard of elligibility for care. Veterans should not have to prove that they are going to harm themselves or others to become elligible for therapy.

Since the time when this book took place, there have been numerous test pilots of programs designed to make the process to become elligible for care easier. One such program is "PSP," or Personalized Support for Progress. Originally designed for women with depression, Personalized Support for Progress is a program specifically for female veterans that provides them with a Peer Supporter—a fellow veteran, not a doctor—to help them get the help they need. Veterans participate in a card-sorting activity with cards printed with specific needs on them that get sorted according to their need for assistance in that area.

According to Life's Challenges: Personalized Support for Progress Randomized Clinical Pilot Trial in a Veterans Health Administration Women's Clinic, "60% [of] elligible, randomized veterans [completed >2 sessions, completed their card sort, developed an action plan, and completed >=1 follow up session]" (Johnson et al. 1600). While this technically didn't meet the standards set in the study, it shows that people in the program tended to stay with it and get the help they needed.

If Jolene had been in such a program, rather than being told that they were "backlogged," and that "a lot of soldiers needed help" (Hannah 313), she would have been able to actually obtain a counselor or therapist, or at least have a person vouching for her needs from inside.
